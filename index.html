How does this work?

A QR code at error-correction level H is built to survive up to ~30% damage — that tolerance is a budget, and PixelQR spends it to make one region of the code fully yours: a writable canvas where every cell is pinned to your drawing (ink dark, background light). The code's own random cells are pushed outside the canvas into the ring — they're what keeps every link unique, and they never mix with your art. What you paint is what scans.

  • Writable canvas (dashed outline) — a rounded-square region sized from the available code budget, guaranteed to end up 100% free of random code cells. Bigger versions buy a bigger canvas (v3 ≈ 13×13 cells, v4 ≈ 17×17, v5 ≈ 19×19, v6 ≈ 23×23 for this link length; a shorter link buys more). If a pattern is stubborn, the canvas steps one cell smaller until the guarantee holds — it never shows noise.
  • The code ring — everything outside the canvas (faint diagonal) is the QR's machine zone: finder patterns, timing, and the random data cells carrying your link plus the digital signatures that make it decode. Its randomness is what makes the code unique.
  • Free payload bits — the pad codewords after the payload terminator are never read by decoders. The app solves them with linear algebra (same family of trick as QArt/QArtText codes) to pin another batch of canvas cells exactly — 16 free bits at v3, 96 at v4, 176 at v5 — spending no scan margin at all.
  • Styled rendering — dots or rounded tiles with rounded finders, the look commercial design-QR generators use to calm the noise down. Every style is verified by decoding the styled render itself (crisp and blurred), so the pretty version is the scannable version.
  • Sacrificed codewords — every 8 module bits form one codeword. Flipping the codeword's bits costs the decoder only one error, so each sacrificed codeword pins a whole batch of canvas cells.
  • Pattern-fit search — the popup pattern is determined by the link and the mask. The app quietly tries variant encodings and all 8 masks, then keeps the one whose free cells fit your drawing best (the tiny #x fragment it may add to your link only changes the anchor, not the page).
  • Live verification — after every edit, the code is decoded in your browser with a real QR decoder (jsQR), both crisp and blurred/shrunk to simulate scanning at a distance. If it fails, the flip budget is auto-trimmed until it passes.
  • Red dots in the canvas mark ink that didn't fit the budget — that's your cue to switch to a higher version (bigger canvas) or simplify that area.

Everything runs in your browser — no server, no uploads. Built on qrcode-generator (MIT) and jsQR (Apache-2.0). Point your phone's camera at the preview to scan it.
